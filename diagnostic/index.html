// where the money leaks

The problem isn't that you don't know your process. It's that nobody has time to look.

You're running batches. Your cellar log lives in a spreadsheet, or three spreadsheets, or a clipboard by the tanks. You already suspect where it's soft — the attenuation that drifts a few points and nobody can say why, the fermenter that always runs a degree warm, the yeast you're repitching a generation or two past where you should, the DO pickup at transfer you've never actually measured.

But suspecting isn't knowing. And knowing isn't the same as having it written down, ranked by cost, and fixed. So it doesn't get fixed. You find out a batch drifted after it's already in the tank. The loss gets folded into “that's brewing,” and everyone goes back to running the next one.

That gap — between what your numbers could tell you and what anyone on your team has time to pull out of them — is where the money leaks out of a brewery. Quietly, one batch at a time.

// what I look for

What a diagnostic typically turns up

These are the kinds of things I look for — not claimed results, but the failure modes that hide in a working brewery's data:

01

Yeast pitched by habit instead of by viable cell count — under- or over-pitching that shows up later as attenuation drift, sluggish starts, or off-flavors. (It's why I built the free pitch-rate and viability calculators — this is that thinking, applied to your operation.)

02

Repitching tired yeast a generation or two too far because nobody's tracking viability or generation count — the invisible cause of “why was that batch different?”

03

A fermenter or two running off-target on temperature and nobody catching it until the ester or fusel profile shows up in the glass.

04

Crash and dump timing run on the calendar instead of on gravity — dropping bright too early, or losing yeast you could have repitched.

05

Tank utilization gaps — a fermenter sitting empty two days a turn is lost production you're paying rent and payroll on.

06

DO pickup at transfer or packaging quietly cutting shelf life — stale beer, returns, and dumped inventory you can't trace to a cause.

I rank whatever I find by what it's costing you, so the report is a sequence to work through — not a wish list.

// common questions

Common questions

“I already know where my problems are.”

You know where some of them are. The value isn't a stranger explaining your own brewery to you — it's the outside pass that names the soft spots, ranks them by dollars, and hands you the fix, so the thing you've been meaning to get to for six months is finally written down and prioritized. A fresh set of eyes on your own data almost always turns up at least one thing you'd stopped seeing.

“My data's a mess.”

Normal. I work with whatever you have — spreadsheets, brew sheets, paper, a photo of the tank-side whiteboard. The gaps in your records are frequently where the losses hide, so a mess isn't a problem for me. It's often the first finding.

“How do I know you won't just tell me things I can't use?”

Two reasons. The fix list is ranked by payoff, so you get a sequence, not a lecture. And the guarantee: if it isn't worth the fee in your judgment, you don't pay it.

“Is my information confidential?”

Yes. I'll sign your NDA, or send you mine. Nothing I see leaves the engagement, ever.

“What if I want you to build the fix?”

Some people do — a live dashboard of every active batch against target, alerting before a batch goes sideways, automation on top of the data you already keep. That's a separate conversation and a separate quote. The diagnostic stands entirely on its own; you're never obligated to go further.
